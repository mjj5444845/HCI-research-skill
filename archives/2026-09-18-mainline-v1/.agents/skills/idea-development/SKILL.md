# Idea Development

## Default starting point
Prefer:
interesting real phenomenon -> deeper question -> unresolved problem -> literature/gap -> RQ

Do not force every idea to start from a paper-shaped gap.

## Workflow

### 1. Phenomenon check
Assess:
- intrinsically interesting?
- real-world relevance?
- deeper mechanism/tension likely?
- not merely a fashionable technology application?

### 2. Non-obviousness attack
Ask:
> If the expected result is obtained, what new knowledge exists?

Prefer questions about:
- mechanism
- boundary condition
- trade-off
- contradiction
- breakdown
- appropriation
- unintended consequence
over simple `X improves Y`.

### 3. Literature handoff
Use Literature Investigation to validate what is actually known.

### 4. Gap validation
Reject manufactured gaps and inflated motivation.

### 5. Multi-framing generation
Generate multiple pragmatic framings when relevant:
- CHI / HCI
- CSCW
- HRI
- UIST
- FAccT
- NLP / ACL-style
- AI/ML
- robotics
- cross-cultural/social computing
- AI policy

For each framing, specify:
- RQ shift
- contribution type
- evidence bar
- strongest venue fit
- likely reviewer objection

Do not make the problem bigger merely to sound important.

### 6. Contribution assessment
Allow:
- empirical
- theoretical
- methodological
- system/technical
- design
- dataset
- benchmark
- resource
- conceptual/framework
- social/policy

Judge whether the contribution gives the community genuinely new knowledge/capability/resource.

### 7. Feasibility gate
Strongly penalize:
- difficult/slow user study with low evidentiary payoff;
- inaccessible data;
- method/RQ mismatch.

A strong idea with impossible current execution may be `INCUBATE`, not `PURSUE`.

### 8. Portfolio value
Assess both:
- standalone paper value;
- strategic contribution to a larger research agenda.

### 9. Multi-agent scoring
Do not rely on one agent.
Suggested dimensions:
- phenomenon interest
- real-world importance
- novelty
- non-obviousness
- theoretical potential
- empirical potential
- method feasibility
- data accessibility
- resource efficiency
- ethical complexity
- contribution strength
- venue fit
- strategic portfolio value

### 10. Decision
- PURSUE
- REFRAME
- INCUBATE
- DROP

Expose evidence and disagreements.

## Exit criteria to Study Design
Must have:
- phenomenon/problem
- why it matters
- closest literature
- strongest defensible gap
- RQ(s)
- required evidence
- expected contribution
- non-obviousness rationale
- why now
- community/venue fit
- feasibility

## AMSC Mainline Scoring

When evaluating the user's own mainline ideas, additionally score:

- relation to Meaning
- relation to Grounding
- relation to Convention
- relation to Adaptation
- relation to Embodied Communication
- fit with the current development stage
- contribution to one or more active gaps
- advisor-fit potential
- long-term trajectory value

A high-mainline-fit idea should normally explain more than a direct preference/effect outcome.

Prefer ideas that reveal:
- how meaning emerges;
- how shared shorthand forms;
- how prior interaction changes communication;
- when personalization becomes convention;
- how situated/embodied output changes pragmatic interpretation.
